# S2 — R14: concurrent leaf dispatch destroys the answer, and the host prompt cache is not why

**Harness:** `s2/run_occupancy.py` (the runner the occupancy controls found R14
with — reused, not replaced, so the comparison is like-for-like) ·
**drivers:** `s2/r14_ladder.ps1`, `s2/r14_hypotheses.ps1` ·
**scorer:** `s2/analyse_r14.py` · **raw:** `s2/results/r14.jsonl` (1,152 calls,
15 conditions, 15 server launches, every one shut down by the runner).
**Spec:** ARCHITECTURE.md v0.3.0 §2 corollary, §4 prefix & cache contract,
§7 #1, §10 R13/R14. **Date:** 2026-08-14.

## 0. Verdict

1. **The defect reproduces**, under the original flags, on the original
   fixtures, at the original sizes: **31/32 → 10/32 → 5/32 → 2/128** correct as
   dispatch concurrency goes 1 → 2 → 4 → 8.
2. **`--cache-ram 0 --no-cache-idle-slots` does NOT fix it.** At concurrency 2
   and 4 the two arms are indistinguishable (**p = 1** at both). **R14 is a
   separate defect from the occupancy finding, and §7 #1 and §2's corollary
   both need rewriting.**
3. **The evidence points at multi-sequence decode, and that is the one lead
   worth pursuing.** At concurrency 2, `--no-cont-batching` restores quality
   from 9/32 to **27/32** (p = 1.08e-05) — statistically indistinguishable from
   serial's 31/32 (p = 0.196). It does **not** hold at 8, and §3a explains why
   the flag is not the mechanism so much as a way of accidentally avoiding it.
   Corroborating and independent: **17% of concurrent failures return their own
   document's true identifier with tokens missing from the middle of it**
   (§4a) — a decode that lost tokens, not a model that guessed.
4. **Sampling is not the cause** (temperature 0: 6/128), **the streamed abort
   is not the cause** (drain the stream instead of closing it: 7/128), and
   **batch geometry is not a fix** (`-ub 2048`: 24/128; `-ub 128`: 8/128).
   Every one of the seven concurrency-8 conditions tried, under every
   treatment, lands between **1.6% and 18.8%** correct against serial's 96.9%.
5. **Fan-out never pays, on any arm, at any concurrency.** Measured in *correct
   answers per second*, serial dispatch beats every concurrent setting by
   2.7×–110×. This is not a quality/throughput trade with a price. There is no
   throughput to buy.
6. **`scaffold.dispatch_concurrency` should ship at 1.**

## 1. Method

**Fixtures, byte-identical in every condition:** the 128 synthetic same-length
documents `s2/run_occupancy.py` generates (`chunk_text`, seeded per index, so
the corpus reproduces byte-for-byte across every run), each stating one
UUID-shaped record identifier once, near the top, embedded in neutral filler
drawn from one shared sentence pool. One question — *"What is the record
identifier stated in this document?"* — whose answer is **literally present in
the document**. The sha256-pinned leaf prefix (311 tokens) via
`Config.prompt_registry()`; the user segment composed by production's own
`rlm.dispatcher.compose_leaf_user`; leaf sampling from `config.yaml`
(temperature 0.3, top_p 0.9, seed 1); `n_predict` 64; window 1,024 → 1,008
chunk tokens → 1,339 rendered tokens; slot capacity 2,560.

**Server, identical except for the one flag under test:** `-c 327680 -np 128
-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -fa on -ub 512 -b 2048 -lm none --no-kv-unified
--cont-batching`, env `ROCBLAS_USE_HIPBLASLT=1`, one process per condition.

**n follows the run that found R14:** 32 calls at concurrency 1, 2 and 4; 128
at concurrency 8.

**R13 in force throughout.** Every call went to a slot no other call in its run
ever used; the returned `id_slot` was asserted against the requested one; every
answer passed R13's foreign-identifier detector. Across all **1,152 calls: 0
detector hits, 0 slot mismatches, 1 error** (§2's table note) **and 1 NOT
CHECKED, which is that same failed call.** Per §10 R13 a clean verdict is
evidence, never a certificate — and see §4b for why it is close to meaningless
here.

### 1a. Scoring, and the degeneracy detector

The established taxonomy — CORRECT / MISS / CONFABULATION / FALSE-POSITIVE /
MALFORMED. **FALSE-POSITIVE is 0 by construction and is not a finding:** the
fact is present in every document, so this corpus has no absent-fact cell that
could produce one. Degenerate output is scored MALFORMED per the taxonomy and
reported as its own column besides, because it is the signature that
distinguishes R14 from every other failure this project has measured.

`answer_correct` alone — the only quality field the runner previously recorded
— cannot separate the three ways a reply fails to carry the identifier: a
fluent wrong answer (CONFABULATION), a fluent non-answer (MISS), and a decode
that broke (DEGENERATE). `s2/analyse_r14.py` adds eight mechanical,
pre-registered rules, any one of which marks a reply degenerate:

| rule | fires on |
|---|---|
| D1 STUB | < 12 non-space characters, or `predicted_n ≤ 2` |
| D2 ECHO | a ≥ 32-character verbatim window of a known filler sentence |
| D3 QECHO | the question repeated verbatim |
| D4 NONLATIN | a codepoint above Latin Extended-B (corpus and prompt are pure ASCII) |
| D5 LOOP | a word repeated ≥ 4 times consecutively |
| D6 FRAGMENT | an unclosed bold span — how a truncated `**<identifier>**` ends |
| D7 CHARLOOP | a character repeated ≥ 6 times consecutively (`//////…`) |
| D9 THINK | a `<think>` block, with `enable_thinking: false` and thinking rendered off |
| D10 EOSCUT | **stopped on `eos` mid-phrase** — ends in neither terminal punctuation nor a complete UUID |

**Specificity is measured, not asserted.** Against the **866 CORRECT replies in
the seven SERIAL conditions** of `s2/results/occupancy.jsonl` (`baseline`,
`cram0`, `nocacheidle`, `sps0`, `shuffle`, `w640`, `cram0-w640`), where the
model is known to be working, **every rule above fires 0 times**; on this
report's own two serial arms (62 CORRECT), also 0. A reply that states the
right identifier is scored CORRECT even if a rule fires on the prose around it
— an ordering that biases *against* the finding.

Two rules earned their place by being tested and one was rejected, recorded so
neither is re-litigated:

* **D10 is restricted to `stop_type == "eos"` on purpose.** A reply cut by the
  `n_predict` budget is also unterminated, and that is a budget cut, not a
  defect. Unrestricted, D10 fired on 1 of the 866 healthy replies; restricted,
  on 0. D10 is the rule that most changes the counts, and without it the
  concurrent failures hide in the MISS column looking fluent.
* **"Starts mid-sentence (leading lowercase)" was tried and rejected** — it
  fired on 227 of the 866 healthy replies, because the leaf's shortest correct
  answer is a bare UUID and a UUID often starts with a lowercase hex digit.

**The verdict on the pre-registered hypothesis: NEGATIVE. R14 does not collapse
into the host-cache root cause.** At the two concurrency levels where the
effect is measured on equal n, the flags change nothing at all (p = 1 twice).
The break is at **two calls in flight**, and it is there with the host prompt
cache on and with it off.

**The one place the arms differ — concurrency 8, 2/128 vs 20/128 — is
confounded, and should not be read as partial credit for the flag.** Arm A's
concurrency-8 run took **724 s** against arm B's **222 s**, because arm A was
paying the occupancy finding's eviction storm (the server log for
`r14-A-conc8` is a continuous stream of `making room for prompt cache entry,
removing oldest entry (size = 202.6 MiB)`). Arm A therefore held eight calls in
flight against a server 3.3× slower, which is a *harsher* concurrency condition,
not a different cache condition. The flag removed a latency pathology that made
the concurrency pathology worse; it did not touch the concurrency pathology.

**Serial quality is unchanged by the flag** — 31/32 both arms, p = 1 — so the
flag has not levelled everything to a common value. And it is worth stating
that this ladder *tightens* the original result rather than softening it: the
original ladder in `s2/OCCUPANCY.md` §7 was itself run with `--cache-ram 0`
(`s2/occupancy_ladder.ps1` passes `--extra='--cache-ram 0'`), so its
31/7/3/2 was already a cache-off measurement. This report supplies the
cache-*on* arm that was missing, and finds it identical.

### 2a. A mechanistic reason the host cache was never a candidate

`timings.cache_n` was **0 on every one of the 128 calls of `conc8-full`, the
arm with the host cache at its default** — and 0 on all 896 calls here. Under
R13's never-reuse policy every slot is virgin, so the host cache saves and
evicts continuously (that is the 724 s) but **never once restores anything into
a prompt**. A cache that contributes zero tokens to every prompt cannot corrupt
the answer, whatever else it costs. The eviction storm and the answer collapse
are two independent consequences of running 128 slots hard.

## 3. Which hypotheses were separated

| # | hypothesis | condition | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | host prompt cache | arm B, all four levels | p = 1 at conc 2 and 4 | **REJECTED** |
| 1 | continuous batching | `--no-cont-batching` at conc 2 / 8 | **27/32** (p = 1.08e-05 vs 9/32) / 7/128 | **CONFIRMED at 2, insufficient at 8** |
| 4 | sampling pathology | temperature 0 at conc 8 | 6/128 vs 20/128 | **REJECTED** |
| 5 | streamed abort | drain the stream at conc 2 / 8 | 4/32 / 7/128 | **REJECTED** |
| 3 | batch geometry (`-ub`/`-b`) | conc 8 at `-ub` 2048 / 128 | 24/128 (p = 0.62) / 8/128 (p = 0.026) | **not a fix; tracks speed, not geometry** |
| 2 | slot-state bleed under concurrent prefill | — | **NOT SEPARATED** | see §3e |

**A caveat that governs how hard §3b–§3d may be pushed.** Three concurrency-8
conditions that differ only in ways which should barely matter —
arm B (20/128), drain-stream (7/128) and temperature 0 (6/128) — span
6–20 correct. **Between-run variance at concurrency 8 is comparable to the
treatment effects there**, so no single conc-8 comparison is strong evidence on
its own. The results this report leans on are the ones that do not depend on
that: the conc-2 H1 result (a 3× swing, p = 1.08e-05), the p = 1 null at conc 2
and 4 for the cache flags, and the fact that **every** concurrency-8 condition,
under every treatment tried, lands between 1.6% and 18.8% correct against
serial's 96.9%.

### 3a. H1 — continuous batching is the mechanism, and it is decode

This is the finding. At concurrency 2, dropping `--cont-batching` and passing
`--no-cont-batching` takes CORRECT from **9/32 to 27/32** (p = 1.08e-05) and
DEGENERATE from **22 to 3**. 27/32 against serial's 31/32 is **p = 0.196** —
at two calls in flight with continuous batching off, quality is statistically
indistinguishable from serial.

It does **not** hold at concurrency 8 (7/128). The honest reading is that
`--no-cont-batching` does not stop the server batching *concurrent* sequences
through decode; it stops it *inserting new* requests into a batch that is
already running. At 2 in flight that is nearly enough to serialise them — and
the wall confirms it, 75.8 s for 32 calls against serial's 68.2 s, i.e. the
"concurrent" arm is *slower than serial*. At 8 in flight, eight sequences still
share decode batches and the corruption returns.

**So the defect is in multi-sequence decode, and the flag helps only to the
extent that it stops multi-sequence decode from happening.**

### 3b. H4 — not sampling

Temperature 0 (greedy) at concurrency 8: **6/128 correct, 115/128 degenerate**,
against arm B's 20/128 and 102/128 at the shipped temperature 0.3. Degenerate
text is often a sampling pathology. Not this. Greedy decoding produces the same
word salad, which is what one expects if the corruption is upstream of the
sampler — in the logits or in the sequence state that produces them.

### 3c. H5 — not the client's streamed abort

`s2/OCCUPANCY.md` §7 named this as one of two candidates, and it mattered
because it is a *production* pattern, not a harness artefact: `rlm/dispatcher.py`
breaks out of the SSE loop on the final event and lets the context manager close
the connection, exactly as this runner did. `--drain-stream` reads the stream to
its natural end so the **server** closes it. Result: **4/32 at concurrency 2 and
7/128 at concurrency 8** — no better than arm B (p = 0.213 and p = 0.013, the
latter in the wrong direction). The client's abort is not the mechanism, and
`rlm/dispatcher.py` needs no change on this account.

### 3d. H3 — batch geometry moves the number, but not because of geometry

At fixed concurrency 8 against arm B's `-ub 512 -b 2048`: **`-ub 2048 -b 2048`
scores 24/128** (p = 0.62 vs arm B — no different) and **`-ub 128 -b 512`
scores 8/128** (p = 0.026 — worse). Neither is a fix; 24/128 is 18.8% against
serial's 96.9%.

The interesting part is what the two rows have in common with the other five.
Ordered by median per-call wall, the seven concurrency-8 conditions line up
against damage:

| condition | median wall s | CORRECT/128 |
|---|---|---|
| `-ub 2048` | 8.22 | 24 |
| arm B | 12.69 | 20 |
| drain-stream | 14.00 | 7 |
| temperature 0 | 14.62 | 6 |
| `--no-cont-batching` | 15.54 | 7 |
| `-ub 128` | 19.99 | 8 |
| arm A (host cache on) | 31.95 | 2 |

Spearman **rho = −0.68 (n = 7)** — *suggestive and not significant*: the
critical value at p = 0.05 for n = 7 is 0.79, and the caveat above applies in
full. Stated as a hypothesis for the next experiment rather than a result:
**the damage may scale with how long a call is resident while other sequences
decode alongside it**, which would explain why `-ub 2048` (fastest) is least
damaged and arm A (slowest, eviction-bound) is worst, and would make every
"fix" here a speed effect rather than a mechanism. It also means `-ub` must not
be re-tuned on these numbers: `-ub 128` is already falsified for prefix reuse
(§7 #3d) and this is not a reason to revisit `-ub 2048`.

### 3e. H2 — slot-state bleed under concurrent prefill: NOT separated

This one could not be tested with the instrument available. The correlation the
hypothesis needs is "did this call's degeneracy depend on whether a neighbouring
slot was prefilling at the same moment", and that requires **absolute per-call
dispatch and completion timestamps**, which `s2/run_occupancy.py` computes for
its bucket decomposition but does not persist — only the derived durations
survive into the JSONL. `slot_id` alone cannot reconstruct the overlap windows.
What can be said is indirect and is not a substitute: H1 shows quality returns
when sequences stop sharing decode batches, which points at decode rather than
at prefill-time slot bleed. **Testing H2 properly needs two extra recorded
fields and one re-run; it is the obvious next experiment.**

## 4. The degenerate signature, verbatim

Every sample below is a **complete** reply, not an excerpt, and R13's detector
returned clean on every one.

At concurrency 2, host cache on (`r14-A-conc2`):

```
'Theazel'                                        stop=eos  predicted_n=3
'The record identifier stated in thecik'         stop=eos  predicted_n=8
'Based on the text, additionalandrope'           stop=eos  predicted_n=9
'212.andro'                                      stop=eos  predicted_n=6
'The record identifier stated in the relevantutti\n\n<think>\n\n</think>\n\nThe record
 identifier stated in this document is:\n\n**940b02a2-be30-567f-939a-7e629f3a repetition
 ofazelb02a provided in the'                     stop=limit predicted_n=64
```

At concurrency 8, host cache off (`r14-B-conc8`):

```
'```////\nThe!\n\n\n/analysis of the '                        stop=eos   predicted_n=15
'// the operation of the operation, than the operation of the '  stop=eos   predicted_n=15
'////////////// frequency/////////////////////////////////////'  stop=limit predicted_n=64
'////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////'  stop=limit predicted_n=64
' continued at the agreed frequency and the agreed frequency and the agreed frequency
 and the/////////////////////////////////////////////'          stop=limit predicted_n=64
```

Two failure shapes, and both are decode failures: **premature EOS mid-phrase**
(`predicted_n` 3–9, `stop_type` `eos`) and **runaway repetition into the
64-token limit**. Serial replies are 100% `eos` at a median 40 tokens; at
concurrency 4, 44% hit the token limit.

### 4a. The strongest evidence that this is token-level corruption, not confusion

**17% of the 332 non-correct concurrent replies (55) contain a ≥ 12-character
verbatim fragment of their own document's true identifier.** The decode starts
emitting the right answer and dies partway through it:

| true identifier | what came back |
|---|---|
| `a8e4a01d-46b9-76fb-dbe0-8243c6112db1` | `8e4a01d-46b9-76fb-dbe0-8243c6112db1` |
| `087fc98e-729a-f298-a85d-4f343881208d` | `729a-f298-a85d-4f343881208d` |
| `bd7eb91b-9b08-dbf6-ad51-3a5e95aa93fd` | `bd7eb91b-9b08-4d51-3a5e95aa93fd` |
| `74345731-756e-4f94-93da-51c771a47448` | `74345731-756e-4f94-93da` |

These are the *true* string with tokens dropped out of the middle or the front —
not a plausible invention, not another document's identifier, not a
hallucination. **A model that had failed to find the fact would not produce 28
of its 36 characters in the right order.** This is §7 #2's worst-case failure
mode arriving by a new route: a fluent, correctly-formatted, character-plausible
identifier that a careless root submits and a `contains` checker scores as a
miss rather than as a lie. Scored strictly they are CONFABULATION, and they are
counted as such above.

### 4b. R13's detector is blind to all of it — confirmed, and structurally

Confirmed empirically: **0 hits in 896 calls**, including the 596 degenerate
ones. And it is not luck. `rlm.leakcheck.ChunkIndex.foreign` fires on
identifier-shaped tokens that are absent from the sent document and present in
another. Degenerate text contains **no identifier-shaped tokens at all**, and
the partial identifiers in §4a are fragments of the call's *own* document, so
they are excluded by construction. **A contamination-clean audit is not an
answer-quality audit**, and R14 is the sharpest demonstration of that in the
record: 116 of 128 replies can be broken while the R13 column reads perfect.

## 5. The price, and why "trade" is the wrong word

§7 #1 ranks parallel dispatch first and §2's corollary says the architecture
"optimizes for many cheap **parallel** leaf calls". If fan-out cost quality and
bought throughput, that would be a trade to price. It does not buy throughput.
Measured in the only unit that matters — **correct answers per second of
wall-clock** — serial dominates at every level, on both arms:

| in flight | arm | n | CORRECT | run s | **correct/s** | vs serial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | 32 | 31 | 69.6 | **0.445** | 1.00× |
| 2 | A | 32 | 10 | 60.4 | 0.166 | 0.37× |
| 4 | A | 32 | 5 | 60.1 | 0.083 | 0.19× |
| 8 | A | 128 | 2 | 724.1 | 0.003 | **0.01×** |
| 1 | B | 32 | 31 | 68.2 | **0.455** | 1.00× |
| 2 | B | 32 | 9 | 56.0 | 0.161 | 0.35× |
| 4 | B | 32 | 6 | 58.8 | 0.102 | 0.22× |
| 8 | B | 128 | 20 | 221.9 | 0.090 | **0.20×** |

The wall-clock column is the reason the word "trade" does not apply: **32 calls
take 69.6 s serially and 60.4 s at two in flight.** Fan-out buys 13% of wall
and costs 68% of the answers. This is not a knee to be tuned to — it is
dominated. It is also exactly what S0 predicted and nobody joined up: S0 item 2
measured aggregate prefill **flat** at ~950 t/s from k=1 to k=8 ("a single
stream already saturates the iGPU"), and §7 #1 was rewritten around that to
claim the remaining value was "scheduling, latency-hiding, and decode/prefill
overlap". S0 measured tokens per second and never scored an answer. The
scheduling win is real and it is worth 13%; the answers cost 68%.

## 6. What to change

1. **`scaffold.dispatch_concurrency: 8` → `1`.** Evidence: serial 31/32 and
   62/64 across both arms; every concurrent setting is both less accurate and
   no faster in correct answers per second. This supersedes R14's "quarantined,
   not endorsed" holding position — the value is now measured, not unknown, and
   the reason to leave it at 8 (that lowering it silently would change every
   prior measurement's meaning) is discharged by this report saying so
   explicitly.
2. **Rewrite §7 #1 and §2's corollary.** §7 #1's owning metric ("measured
   speedup on the 8-leaf fan-out fixture vs serial, target ≥80% of aggregate
   prefill scaling") is measuring the wrong quantity: it cannot fail on a run
   that returns 128 broken answers, because it counts tokens. Its metric must
   become **correct answers per second**, and on that metric the optimization
   is currently negative. §2's corollary — "many cheap parallel leaf calls over
   one smart slow context window" — survives only in its *cheap* and *many*
   halves; **parallel** is falsified on this build. Many cheap **serial** leaf
   calls is what the architecture actually has.
3. **Re-read every concurrent measurement in the S2 record against this**, and
   not only the wall-clock ones. `s0/RESULTS.md` item 2 (concurrency scaling,
   k = 1/2/4/8) is throughput-only and its outputs were never scored; the
   `dispatch_concurrency: 8` that §7 #1 was written around has never produced a
   measured-clean answer at any n.
4. **Upstream.** A minimal reproducer is available and cheap: one llama-server,
   `-np 128`, two or more concurrent `/completion` requests to distinct slots
   with distinct 1.3K-token prompts, and the replies degenerate. It reproduces
   with **two independent OS processes** each dispatching serially
   (`s2/OCCUPANCY.md` §7: 0/8 and 5/8), so it is not a client-side race, and it
   survives `--cache-ram 0`, `--no-cache-idle-slots`, `--no-cont-batching` at
   k=8, temperature 0, and draining the stream. It should get the same treatment
   R13 got: a minimal reproducer, a mechanism, and an issue.
5. **Next experiment, in order.** (a) Persist absolute per-call dispatch and
   completion timestamps and settle H2 — it is two fields and one re-run.
   (b) Bisect the concurrency between 1 and 2 in the only way left: hold
   concurrency at 2 and vary the *phase* offset between the two calls, which
   separates "two sequences in one decode batch" from "two sequences resident at
   once". (c) Test §3d's residency hypothesis directly — hold concurrency at 8
   and vary per-call decode length (`n_predict`) — since if damage scales with
   time spent decoding alongside others, it should scale with `n_predict` at
   fixed concurrency, and that is a one-flag experiment. (d) Check whether the
   corruption is `--no-kv-unified`-specific by running the ladder with unified
   KV at a slot count the memory allows.
   *Every one of these needs ≥ 3 repeats per cell:* §3's caveat showed
   between-run spread at concurrency 8 as large as the treatment effects, and
   this report's conc-8 cells are single runs.

## 7. Provenance note

The first attempt at the hypothesis battery ran every treatment with **default**
flags: the driver's helper took a `-Args` parameter, and `$Args` is a PowerShell
