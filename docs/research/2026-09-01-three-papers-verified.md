## 1. The answer to the question that prompted this

**Code World Model was correctly skipped — and "we misclassified it" is too soft a verdict, in our own favour and the paper's. The precise charge is that the paper pitches itself as agent-training infrastructure, twice, and never executes the pitch.**

Both pitches, verbatim [V]. §1 Introduction: *"These capabilities are essential for building worlds that evolve coherently beyond predefined trajectories and support open-ended interaction; such worlds, in turn, provide a foundation for game generation and for training and evaluating agents [5, 7, 8, 9]."* §3.3 Data, closing the case for real video: *"Real-video data can make generated visual environments closer to an agent's deployment distribution, allowing Code World Model to support not only virtual-world generation but also scalable real-world visual environments for training and evaluating agents."* Neither sentence is attached to an agent, an environment interface, an evaluation, or a number; both are the paper's own framing of what its output is *for*.

Say both halves of this. Our filing error is **understandable** — the paper solicits it, in its introduction and again in its method, and an indexer reading either sentence would file it where we filed it. And it is **still our error**, because both sentences are promissory on their face, the Limitations retract the agentic claim outright (below), and the arXiv primary category is cs.CV. The charge against the paper is the heavier one: it is a cs.CV video-generation conditioning paper that borrows the agent-infrastructure frame without paying for it.

It is a cs.CV video-generation conditioning paper. It contains **zero quantitative results of any kind** [V] — not one agent-capability number and, despite a section titled "4.2 Visual Quality Results", not one visual-quality metric either. Verified three independent ways: grep over the LaTeXML text, over a `pdftotext -layout` extraction, and over a fresh `pypdf` extraction, all returning zero for `Table N`, FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, "user study", "ablation", "baseline", and the percent character. The one hole that would defeat a text grep — a table rendered as an image — was closed by a recursive raster-XObject census of all 20 pages: **pages 1, 3, 9 and 10 carry the only images (2, 1, 4 and 5 respectively); every other page carries none** [V]. There is no hidden table.

The entire empirical content is one figure of five qualitative prompts (A–E), six frames each = 30 generated RGB frames [V], plus a one-frame KITTI-360 proof of concept. The only comparison to prior work is qualitative, lives off-paper on a project page, and explicitly excludes the axis on which the method is worse: *"To isolate control quality, the comparisons do not consider inference latency."* [V] The paper never claims to have measured anything — "preliminary evidence" appears twice, and the Conclusions say "qualitative evaluations" [V]. The honest charge is therefore **"harness claimed, measurement never attempted or promised"**, not "harness claimed, never measured."

**The paper's sharpest evidence against itself is internal, and it is stronger than any absence grep we ran.** A missing word is a claim about search; a contradiction between two sentences of the same v1 needs no methodology at all. Contributions list, first bullet [V]:

> *"We introduce Code World Model, a framework in which a coding agent governs long-horizon world evolution through executable code, while a video model renders the evolving world state into high-fidelity visual observations."*

§5 Limitations, same paper, same version [V]:

> *"Second, despite their rapid progress, current coding agents still struggle to implement highly complex game mechanisms reliably from scratch. Our prototype therefore does not demonstrate autonomous construction of a complete open-world game or simulator."*

Governing long-horizon world evolution is the contribution; not demonstrating autonomous construction is the limitation. The paper is claiming in its contributions the exact capability it disclaims four sections later, and the gap between them is the whole of what would have made it a harness paper. That settles the classification without appealing to a single grep.

And §4.1 settles what the "coding agent" actually is [V]: *"During inference, we provide it with existing game-engine code that supplies basic player controls, collision handling, and a runtime update loop… We run this world under user control, record the resulting proxy video, and provide that sequence as a frame-wise condition to the downstream video model."* A **human plays** the agent-built world; the proxy is recorded offline; the agent is out of the loop during generation. The §3.1 agent-code loop the paper's thesis rests on is never run.

**Three things were left on the table, and none of them is a number.** They are worth ten minutes, not a re-read:

1. **§2.3 is the most useful page in the paper for this project** [V] — a prior-art cluster on harness-owned executable state that nothing else in the set cites: WorldCoder [67] (an LLM agent writes a Python world model and refines it through environment interaction), Dainese et al. 2. **CWM's own state diagram is an anti-pattern worth recording.** Fig. 2 — recovered as a 1672×941 JPEG nested in the page-3 form XObject, and read from pixels rather than from the caption — shows **three writers into World State**: the coding agent writes it directly (`update`), Code writes it (`update`), and the **video model writes it** (`update`) [V]. A first lens read the arrow directions off the caption instead of the figure and reported a disciplined single-writer path; that reading is **refuted**. Eq. 2 and Eq. 4 confirm it in the formalism — state decomposes as `S_t = (S_exe, S_vis)` with `S_vis_{t+1} ~ G_theta(S_vis_t, S_exe_{t+1})`, so half of "world state" is produced by the generative model. 3. **The delegate's capability ceiling is designed around rather than measured** [V]. Fig. 3's caption: *"to ensure that the coding agent can easily reproduce the proxy at inference time, we exclude skeletal pose, detailed 3D models, and other representations that are difficult for coding agents to construct and maintain."* And §3.2: *"Current coding agents still struggle to implement such joint-level motion control reliably, which can limit final performance."* The interface was narrowed to what the delegate can do, and the narrowing was never priced. ## 2. Code World Model — what it actually measures, and the two confounds that matter

**Nothing measured. Configuration only.** Every number in the paper is a hyperparameter or a data-collection count [V]: 157 gameplay takes, ≈5.6 h source video, 9,420 five-second clips at a 2 s stride; targets 124 frames at 1344×768 / 24 FPS; proxy 124 frames at 336×192; 11 proxy frames into the multimodal encoder at offsets 0, 12, …, 120; rank-128 LoRA across all 50 blocks ≈ 596 M trainable parameters; 8× H800; global batch 8; 3 epochs = 3,534 steps; LR 2e-5 → 1e-6 after a 100-step warmup; 20 explicit-Euler sampling steps.

Fig. 4's caption says "only five hours of GTA V gameplay" against §4.1's "approximately 5.6 hours" [V]. A first lens called this a flattering round-down; **that reading is not licensed** — 9,420 clips at a 2 s stride covers 5.23 h of source timeline, giving "five hours" a benign reading. What *is* worth stating is the exposure: 9,420 × 5 s = 13.08 clip-hours drawn from 5.6 h of source, so each second of source is seen ≈2.34× [V-derived]. "GTA V" is named exactly once in the paper, in that caption; §3.3 Data never names the title and never describes how runtime state was extracted from a commercial game.

**Confound 1 — a second and a third proprietary model in the stack, absent from the abstract** [V]. §4.1: *"For the qualitative examples, GPT Image 2 generates a 1536 × 864 first-frame appearance anchor conditioned on the first proxy frame and a text prompt written by the coding agent… The generated image is resized to 1344 × 768, initializes target latent slot 0, and is supplied to both the multimodal encoder and the Ref2VA appearance-reference branch."* Every showcased frame is seeded by a GPT Image 2 still whose prompt restates the spatial content the proxy is supposed to be carrying. No ablation removes it. The demonstration pipeline is GPT-5.6 Sol + GPT Image 2 + a LoRA-adapted MiniMax-H3 Ref2VA (a public checkpoint, ref [77] "Official GitHub repository") + a human, with nothing isolated.

**Confound 2 — long-horizon identity is carried by a re-pasted still, not by the world state** [V]. §4.1: long video is stitched from overlapping 124-frame windows with a 34-frame overlap advancing 90 frames; *"the same appearance anchor is again supplied"* to every window, and *"reusing the anchor helps preserve global identity and appearance across windows."* The mechanism that actually persists identity across time in the reported runs is a static GPT Image 2 image plus 34 frames of pixel overlap — not the executable world state the whole thesis rests on. No drift metric, no identity-preservation score, no total generated length.

**Two corrections to first readings, both against the prosecutorial direction.** (i) The claimed arithmetic inconsistency between "11 of 124 frames" and "1/16 as many visual tokens" **does not exist**: the paper describes two distinct pathways — 11 subsampled frames into the multimodal encoder, and the complete 124-frame sequence as frame-aligned conditioning — and 336×192 against 1344×768 is exactly 1/16 the pixels. The residual valid finding is much weaker: the injection mechanism for the 124-frame condition is never stated. (ii) The paper *does* assert that agents are becoming more capable, twice — "recent agentic language models and coding agents have become increasingly capable of autonomously generating, modifying, and maintaining complex codebases [71–74]" and §2.3's "the growing ability of coding agents to construct and modify complex interactive programs" — but always as cited background about **other people's systems**, never as its own result [V].

**Noise floor: not applicable.** There are no rows, so the Terminal-Bench retry-inversion shape cannot occur here — and neither can any budgeted comparison. There is no held-out set and no evaluation protocol of any kind to attach a metric to [V].

The Comments field contains solely a project-page URL. **No code, no weights, no data.** Fig. 1 — the figure a social summary reproduces — carries the disclosure *"This figure is AI-generated for illustration purposes."* [V]

## 3. Prime Agent — a disowned headline, an author-declared null, and one mechanism worth stealing

### 3.1 The headline is same-model and is not a controlled comparison, and the paper says so

The abstract: *"Prime Agent raises ARC-AGI-3 RHAE Best@1 from 30% to 95.5%"* [V]. The introduction restates it as **95%** [V]. **RHAE is never defined or expanded anywhere** — it occurs exactly twice, in the abstract and the Fig. 5 caption — and **`Best@1` occurs exactly once**, in the abstract, and is never operationalized [V]. Neither string appears in the released repo.

The full result set exists only in Fig. 5's legend, in two separately-headed columns [V, recovered from the PDF]:

```
RUNS                                    EXTERNAL REFERENCES
Prime Agent + Opus 5        95.5%       Human baseline               95.4%
Prime Agent + GPT-5.6 Sol   78.3%       GPT-5.6 Sol, Responses API   38.3%
Prime Agent + Terra         25.7%       GPT-5.6 Terra, Responses API 13.3%
Prime Agent + GLM 5.2        8.6%       Opus 5, ARC harness          30.2%
Hermes Agent + GPT-5.6 Sol   5.8%       GPT-5.6 Sol, ARC harness      7.0%
```

The model name is the same on both sides of the headline, but the `30.2%` is an **external published value the authors did not produce**. §3.1 body prose disowns the causal reading in the authors' own words [V]:

> *"They are external values because our native-harness reruns fell below the published scores, so they situate the result rather than isolate a causal harness effect."*

**That disclaimer is in body text. Fig. 5's caption disclaims nothing** — it reads, in full: *"ARC-AGI-3 test-time scaling. RHAE score versus output tokens per game (left) and estimated API cost (right)."* [V] The caption that disclaims significance belongs to Table 1, a different result. Our repo's memory placed the disclaimer in the wrong caption.

Worse for reproducibility: the authors **ran** the matched-condition controls and withheld them [V] — *"We note that Claude Code and Codex runs perform worse than Anthropic and Open AI self-reported performance on ARC-AGI-3 (public set), so we defer to their results over our own runs with matched prompt and settings."* **Which** published number replaced their own Opus 5 run is NOT-FOUND: §3.1 cites Anthropic/OpenAI self-reports, while the legend attributes the 30.2% to a third-party "ARC harness". Neither `30.2` nor "ARC harness" appears anywhere in the paper's prose, so a reader of the text alone cannot learn that the baseline is external at all.

### 3.2 The controlled comparisons, and what survives ±7

**Table 1 is the only table in the paper**, and its caption is the discipline [V]: *"Bold marks the higher point estimate within each nominal-model pair; metrics differ by row. Bold is not statistical significance, and uncertainty intervals are unavailable."* Three nominal-model pairs (GLM-5.2 / Pi-mono, Opus 5 / Claude Code, GPT-5.6 Sol / Codex) with `reasoning: high` on both arms, × 9 rows = 27 within-model comparisons. No n per row except EmulatorBench (16 reconstructions). Prime Agent **wins 20, loses 7** [V].

Margins, computed from the published cells [V-derived]:

| column | mean margin | W/L | tightest win |
|---|---|---|---|
| GLM-5.2 vs Pi-mono | **+0.104** | 8/1 | +0.009 |
| Opus 5 vs Claude Code | +0.024 | 6/3 | +0.007 |
| Opus 5 vs Claude Code, dropping LongCoT-Mini | **+0.007** | 5/3 | +0.007 |

### 3.4 What the code says that the paper does not [V, released repo at HEAD `85ac06e`, 2026-09-01]

**The admission path for self-authored artifacts is a schema check and nothing else.** `validateEdit` (`refinement.ts:673–712`) has ten structural branches — action verb, kind, id, title, content, and for skills a reference shape. Zero semantic checks, zero execution. `_validate_python_skill_reference` (`harness.py:129–139`) verifies that `type == "python"` and that the import and callable fields are **non-empty strings** — it never imports the module and never calls the callable, and the kernel's bootstrap import loop iterates *installed* packages, not refinement-authored entries. A skill naming a module that does not exist passes, persists, and is surfaced in every later system prompt.

A first reading called the auto-refine review a second gate on proposed artifacts. **That is wrong, and the truth is worse**: `AUTO_REFINE_REVIEW_SYSTEM_PROMPT` decides *whether `/refine` runs at a checkpoint*, before any edit exists, and returns a `shouldRefine: boolean`. Once refinement runs, every artifact is admitted on `validateEdit` alone. Auto-refine defaults to **on** (`enabled: this.settings.autoRefine?.enabled ?? true`), at a 25-turn interval with a 20-minute cooldown.

Two real harness-owned guards do exist and should be credited: `base_system_prompt` is not editable, and `applyRefinementProposal` keeps a `before` snapshot and bumps a per-entry `version`, refusing an edit whose entry changed during planning.

**The model marks its own work done.** §2.6: *"A goal retains an objective across continuations and ends through agentic completion, when the agent marks the goal complete."* `goal.complete()` takes no arguments and carries no evidence payload; the host handler's entire guard is that a goal exists. The only protection is prose, repeated four times in `goals.ts`. That is the exact inverse of the Recuris ledger property, shipped in the same harness that also ships the next item.

**The harness computes its own audit row on one path and hands the model a forger on the other. This is the closest published analogue of our audit-row hole, and nothing in the paper mentions either path.** Every refinement writes a four-field record — `trigger`, `changes`, `evidence`, `outcome` (`HarnessRefinementEvent`, `refinement.ts:50-57`).

On the `/refine` path the model's proposal JSON has **no `changes` field at all** (the exact shape it is told to output is `refinement.ts:155-173`: `summary`, `rationale`, `expectedOutcome`, `edits[]`). The harness derives that field itself, from the edits that actually applied, and writes the record (`refinement.ts:791-799`):

```ts
const changes = appliedEdits.filter((edit) => edit.applied).map((edit) => `${edit.action} ${edit.kind}:${edit.id}`);
state.refinements.push({ id: options.id, trigger: proposal.summary, changes, evidence: proposal.rationale, outcome: proposal.expectedOutcome, created_at: now() });
```

So exactly **one field of the four is harness-owned** — the one that names what happened. The model still authors the surrounding three.

The Python kernel then hands that one field back. `rlm.harness.record_refinement(trigger, changes, evidence=..., outcome=...)` builds the identical `RefinementEvent` with `changes` taken verbatim from the caller and appended with no cross-check against any applied edit (`harness.py:677-702`); a bare string is accepted and silently wrapped — `normalized_changes = [changes] if isinstance(changes, str) else list(changes)` — with a test pinning that behaviour (`test/test_harness.py:911`, `test_record_refinement_accepts_single_change_string`). This is **not** an internal API the model cannot reach: `record_refinement` is on the kernel's bootstrap allowlist (`kernel/bootstrap.ts:51`) and is advertised to the model by name in the RLM system prompt (`prompts/rlm.ts:47`). Both rows land in the same `state.refinements` list and are rendered into later prompts by the same formatter (`refinement.ts:508-513`), **with no marker distinguishing the derived row from the declared one**.

A harness that computes its own audit row on one path and lets the model write that same field as free text on a second path is exactly the design question our 2-audit-rows-for-3-spent-decisions gap is asking — answered twice, and inconsistently, inside one shipped harness.

**The one mechanism worth stealing outright.** Autonomous quality gates run as **host shell commands checked by exit code** (`result.status === 0 && !result.error && !result.timedOut`), carry an explicit per-command `maxRetries`, and — the part we do not have — refuse to re-run a failed gate when the git worktree fingerprint is unchanged: *"not rerun: workspace unchanged since previous failed gate. Edit source files, tests, or a blocker artifact before attempting to finish again."* A no-work-no-retry primitive, in working code. The caveat: `DEFAULT_AUTONOMOUS_GATES.commands` is `[]`, so **by default autonomous mode has no end-condition test at all** and stops only on limits (3 continuations, 12 turns, 80,000 tokens, 30 minutes). The paper never states which values the ARC runs used.

**Delegation primitives the paper never evaluates.** `rlm()` is deliberately fire-and-forget — *"admission returns a child handle immediately… Results arrive only through an available messaging capability or files, never as an `rlm()` return value"*, and Appendix B: *"The explicit reply path is intentional: a child is a persistent concurrent session, not a stateless completion returned by rlm."* (The README contradicts this; the paper is correct.) A child can run a **different model** (`model` takes an exact `provider/model` selector, `thinking` sets the child's reasoning level) with a bounded catalog search — the direct analogue of our root-27B / leaf-35B-A3B split, and **no experiment in the paper uses it**. Cost accounting rolls descendants into the parent's billable total while restoring the parent's context total, so *"delegation remains visible in test-time cost"* — asymmetric, since Prime Agent's plotted cost includes its subagents and the paper never says whether any comparison harness spawns any.

**Drift, not conflict.** The paper says each session owns a persistent **IPython** REPL; repo HEAD has replaced the Jupyter/ipykernel client with a minimal CPython REPL while keeping the tool named `ipython`. The change is in `[Unreleased]`, above release 0.8.1 dated 2026-08-26, so the paper was accurate on 2026-08-24.

**Neither released repo contains evaluation code for any benchmark in the paper.** `grep -ril` for arc-agi, RHAE, oolong, pmpp, emulatorbench, mazebench, factorio and nanogpt returns zero files across the prime-agent tree (1,269 files; packages `agent`, `ai`, `coding-agent`, `tui`); the linked `verifiers` repo (482 files, fetched unauthenticated) likewise returns zero. "Code is available" means the harness, not the experiments.

### 4.2 The outer loop — the mechanism, which is genuinely good, and the evidence, which is absent

**Eq. 6, verbatim** [V]: `Accept(H'_{t+1}) ⟺ J_train(H'_{t+1}) > J_train(H_t) ∧ J_dev(H'_{t+1}) ≥ J_dev(H_t)`, with *"If the condition holds, the meta-harness promotes H'_{t+1} to H_{t+1}; otherwise H_t is retained."* The comparison is against the **immediately preceding accepted state**, which is exactly the discipline our retry-inversion correction obliges. Note it is **credited, not invented** — line 184 attaches "(Nguyen et al., 2026)" to it, and Related Work says *"Recursive Self-Evolving Agents use an independent development split to gate persistent updates."*

Two further mechanism details worth copying verbatim [V]:

> *"Results on the development set are used exclusively by the acceptance gate and are never exposed to P when constructing an update proposal."* … *"Trajectories and scores from the development set are not included in the record."*

> *"Each outer-loop iteration is restricted to exactly one of the five harness components… This restriction keeps credit assignment interpretable, as each gain or regression is attributable to a single coherent intervention rather than to several simultaneous changes."*

The five components are Context and Memory; Tools and Specifications; Execution Runtime; Orchestration; Evaluation and Feedback — and **Orchestration explicitly owns "attempt budgets"**, which is why K = 12 sits inside the treatment.

**The evidence budget behind all of it is NOT-FOUND.** `|D_train|`, `|D_dev|` and `T` appear only as symbols, in the paper, the appendix, Algorithm 1 and the released code. The loop ran 7 days, invoked 224 subagents, recorded *"at least 123 recursive iterations"*, and accumulated **54 harness updates**. **No rejection count is published**, and "recursive iteration" is never defined as an outer-loop iteration, so even the ≤44% acceptance rate is unsafe. The gate is a bare strict inequality with no threshold, no interval and no repeat; conjoined with the dev non-decline it admits roughly a **quarter** of null proposals under symmetric run-to-run noise — not half, as a first reading claimed.

**And there is no negative control of any kind for the outer loop** [V]. §5.2 "Ablation Studies" has exactly two subsections — "Effect of the Design Harness" and "Cost–Performance Trade-off". There is **no H₀-vs-H_T row on PosterBench** anywhere in Tables 1–4: Table 4's "Original" column is the coding agent with *no design harness*, not the initial scaffold. So the +12.40 measures **having a design harness**, not the 54 learned updates. The only trace of the loop's own progress is Fig. 1(a), n = 1 *"one representative paper"*: initial harness 49.00 → self-evolve plateau 80.88 → final 88.39, with a handcrafted-workflow reference line at 56.30. **Those four values appear nowhere in the paper's text**, which evaluator produced them is NOT-FOUND, and 88.39 exceeds every score in every table (best is 82.31). The autonomous stage supplies +31.88 of the +39.39 (81%); the final +7.51 comes from *"Stage 2: Human-in-the-Loop"* — which is precisely where §3.2 permits **R_meta itself** to be revised.

**No matched-budget control.** The treatment carries *"at most K = 12 refinement attempts"* plus a blocking rule validator and a critic VLM; Table 5's fixed-factor list for the ablation is *"Model, code agent, shared PosterBench-mini papers, and frozen PosterBench protocol"* — attempt budget is not in it, and Appendix A.2 says outright *"System-specific orchestration, tools, and repair policies remain part of each system configuration."* The baseline arm's budget is never stated, and the released `harness_matrix.py` runner has **no no-harness mode** (8 coding harnesses, all with DesignHarness), so Table 4's "Original" column is not reproducible from the release.

**The "shared" prompt is not neutral.** The ~2-page "Shared User Prompt (verbatim)" handed to every compared system (PDF pp. 22–23; image-only in the HTML) orders conformance to AutoDesign's own validator DOM contract, naming its internal classes [V]: *"Build visual evidence units in the DOM shape expected by validation. All four selectors are AutoDesign-internal — `.od-layer` is AutoDesign's own renderer class (`schema.py:116`, `deck_html_renderer.py`), not a competitor's, correcting a first reading. ### 4.3 What survives ±7

**The caveat belongs here, before the table, because this section carries the document's strongest conclusion.** The floor is Recuris' own (+0.00 [−6.98, +7.27] on an identical re-run three days apart, Appendix A [V], recorded at `docs/research/2026-08-31-papers-verified.md:57`), and it was measured on a τ²-Retail success rate. Applying it to a 0–100 PosterBench Score is **a transfer, and a longer one than §3.2's**: PosterBench Score is a weighted rubric over seven 0–10 dimensions with a **hard per-poster cap applied before averaging** (Eq. 8), so it is not a success rate and not even a smooth mean — one capped poster moves an n = 10 mean by up to 6 points. That is further from the quantity the floor was measured on than any Table-1 metric §3.2 applied it to. And the **rubric-only column is our reconstruction, not a published number** — it is [V-derived] throughout, so in those two columns the floor is being applied to a quantity we computed from the paper's weights and dimension means, not to one AutoDesign printed.

Which way the transfer errs is undetermined: a Bernoulli success rate carries larger per-item variance than a 0–100 rubric score, which argues the borrowed floor is too wide; Eq. 8's caps make the rubric heavy-tailed, which argues it is too narrow. Neither can be settled without a re-run, and AutoDesign publishes none.

**How much weight the table can bear.** Not "AutoDesign's margins are inside noise" — that claim needs a floor characterised on *this* metric, and no one has one. What it bears is the weaker and still sufficient form: **AutoDesign publishes no interval on any automatic PosterBench score, so those margins are unfalsifiable at the scale where a plausible re-run floor would sit** (it does plot 95% intervals for the four systems in Fig. 9(a)'s human evaluation — see §5(c) — so the absence is scoped to the automatic scores, not to the paper), and under the only re-run floor characterised anywhere in our sources — Recuris', borrowed, not ours — the two Main-Track headlines fail once ceiling avoidance is removed. Read the table as a sensitivity analysis against a borrowed threshold, not as a significance test.

## 5. The three beliefs, adjudicated

### (a) The harness-swap confound — DESTROYED for both papers. Our same-model baselines are not the differentiator.

`2026-08-31-papers-verified.md` §5 conclusion 4 says our B1/B2/B3 baselines being the same model as the RLM arm makes our margins *"immune to the confound that hollows out AutoDesign and Prime Agent, which report a harness swap and a model swap together and call it a harness gain."* **That is false for both papers** [V].

- **AutoDesign.** Table 5's ablation row: fixed = *"Model, code agent, shared PosterBench-mini papers, and frozen PosterBench protocol"*; varied = *"Presence of the AutoDesign design harness"*. §5.2.1: *"we hold the model and coding agent fixed and compare each configuration before and after harness attachment."* Table 1 gives Claude Design's own row the same Coding Agent (Claude Code) and Model (Claude 4.8) columns as AutoDesign's.
- **Prime Agent.** Table 1's caption: *"within each nominal-model pair"*, with `reasoning: high` on both arms of all three pairs. Fig. 8: *"at fixed within-model budgets"*. §3.4: *"In these reported within-model comparisons"*. §3.5 MazeBench: *"We compare Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol with Prime Agent versus their native harnesses."* The ARC headline is Opus 5 on both sides.

Three qualifications keep this from being a clean surrender, and all three are the *real* differentiators:

1. **AutoDesign's Claude Design row is an assertion about a black box.** Claude Design is a closed commercial product; the paper states no method for pinning its model or preventing internal routing. Prime Agent hedges in its own caption — *"nominal-model pair"* — and pins nothing beyond `reasoning: high`. Same model **name**, not same serving stack.
2. **Prime Agent's headline is the one comparison that is not model-matched in any verified sense** — an external third-party value, with the authors' own matched-prompt control run and withheld.
3. **Neither paper matches budget, and both admit it.** AutoDesign's treatment carries K = 12 attempts against an unstated baseline budget, with repair policy explicitly declared part of the treatment. Prime Agent's autonomous mode carries `maxRetries` / `maxContinuations` / `maxTurns` / `maxTokens` and never states the values used for ARC. **Neither publishes an interval on any automatic score, and neither has a characterised null floor.**

**What replaces the belief, for the measurement record — and two of the three replacements are weaker than the sentence they replace.** A first version of this paragraph destroyed one uncited belief and installed three more, none of them carrying a repo line. Each is now cited or marked owed, because a differentiator we cannot cite is not a differentiator.

1. **Matched budget is NOT a live differentiator. It is an obligation adopted after the fact, and S4 did not meet it** [V]. `ARCHITECTURE.md:384` reads *"Deliberately no hard cost gate — the decision rule stays single"*; the arms were not run at equal budget — B1 has its own dedicated serving profile (`ARCHITECTURE.md:391`) — and S4 reports cost as an annotation beside the margin rather than as a controlled variable, at 0.19× / 0.06× / 0.13× the tokens of B1/B2/B3. The confound never bit here because the winning arm was the *cheaper* one, which is luck about the direction, not a control. The rule *"Budget is a controlled variable, pre-registered, held equal across arms"* was written **on 2026-08-31**, in `docs/research/2026-08-31-papers-verified.md:106`, as an obligation on comparisons **not yet run**. **"Pre-registered" is struck from this claim**: this repo pre-registers a great deal — §8's category split, decision rule, inference layer, escalation and B1 overflow policy (`ARCHITECTURE.md:373-391`) — and has never pre-registered budget equality anywhere. 2. **Published intervals are real, with one gap — and the null floor is a different instrument from the intervals** [V]. §8 makes the interval mandatory (`ARCHITECTURE.md:378`: a 10,000-resample task-level paired bootstrap CI stated beside every margin), and S4 published them (`ARCHITECTURE.md:445`: +30 vs B1, p = 0.0000, CI [+1.000, +1.000]; **+13 vs B2, p = 0.0002, CI [+0.256, +0.589]**; +29 vs B3, p = 0.0000, CI [+0.900, +1.000]). ### (b) AutoDesign's agent-fit gradient — the arithmetic stands; the causal reading does not.

**The number.** Verbatim [V]: *"The probability that the benchmark-preferred poster agrees with the human decision rises from 51.9% for 0–3-point gaps to 74.4% for gaps of at least 20 points."* The full ladder, with per-bin n recovered from Fig. 10(b) — which the text omits and the HTML drops [V]:

**The chain is mis-attributed.** The 51.9% characterises **PosterBench**, the frozen external evaluator. R_meta is also not strictly frozen: *"human guidance may be provided to the coding agent responsible for implementing the evaluator… Such evaluator revision requires explicit human input"*, and §A.4 scopes the freeze as *"fixed within each optimization run"* — per-run, implying more than one. The number of revisions is NOT-FOUND.

**Do not map the ladder onto the headline.** A first reading argued that +7.45 falls in the 6–10 bin and is therefore "barely better than a coin flip". **That step is invalid.** Fig. 10(b) bins **individual poster-pair** gaps within the human study; +7.45 is a difference of **100-paper means**, and averaging suppresses exactly the per-item noise the figure prices. The bin counts prove the mismatch: 500 of 919 judged pairs had gaps of 10+ points and 201 had 20+, so per-pair gaps run far larger than mean deltas. **The valid statement is narrower and still damaging: for any single close comparison, PosterBench is statistically indistinguishable from a coin.**

**The human ground truth is itself unstable** [V]. Nominal Krippendorff α = **0.101**, disclosed once in an appendix table and absent from the abstract, §5.3, the conclusion and the repo README. Qualify it honestly: 933 judgments over a 600-task roster is **1.56 ratings per unique item**, so α rests on at most ~333 multiply-rated items, and it is nominal α over a three-category schema in which "approximately equal" was 24–35% of responses — which mechanically depresses it. Poster-level benchmark/human association is r = **0.34 [0.22, 0.44]**, i.e. R² = 0.116. And Fig. 9(a) **does** plot 95% intervals for all four systems (only AutoDesign's endpoints are printed): AutoDesign 64.0 [55.2, 77.8]; Claude Code 51.7 ≈[40, 61]; OpenDesign 43.4 ≈[31, 58]; Claude Design 40.9 ≈[29, 52] [S, read from the figure]. **AutoDesign's interval overlaps Claude Code's**, and OpenDesign's upper bound exceeds AutoDesign's lower — so *"highest human preference among evaluated systems"* is not separated at 95% from the bare Claude Code baseline it exists to beat.

A blind reviewer glancing at a pairwise comparison weighs exactly the two it loses. That is *why* the human agreement is weak, and it is a stronger and more transferable finding than the 51.9% alone.

## 6. What this changes here

**Delegation (237 successful RLM episodes; 15 delegate at all, 6 substantively).** Prime Agent's Factorio run is the only quantitative delegation datum in any of these papers: **633 depth-one subagents, 149 dispatch waves, max 7 concurrent, 23.4 M output tokens, seven days — recursion depth 1, n = 1, no delegation-ablated arm, and the figure's own caption calls it a curated pick.** **Put it beside ours and it is not a bracket on any variable** — and calling it one repeats the exact inference §5(a) spends a whole section destroying: one factor at a time with everything else swapped. Their side is n = 1, curated by the figure's own caption, on a frontier model, in a different environment, at a seven-day horizon; ours is 237 episodes on a local 27B root over minute-scale tasks. Horizon is **one candidate explanation among several** — environment, model class, task irreducibility and the tool surface all moved at the same time. And our side is additionally faulted at the source of the very counts being cited: `ARCHITECTURE.md:499` (R16), where the 237/15/6 figures live, says *"the heavily-delegating episodes on record all failed, but under a known `slot_pool_error_drained` fault, so delegation's quality is **unmeasured**, not bad"* [V]. A faulted measurement is not one arm of a bracket.

So the honest form is a **hypothesis, not a finding: horizon may elicit delegation where corpus size does not** — worth designing a test for, and licensing no benchmark-v2 design constraint on its own. R16's mitigation column already fixes the order — *"Benchmark v2 first (tasks unanswerable without delegating; train/held-out split), then the rotation fault"* — and the property it names as the thing v2 must force is, in its own words, *"tasks unanswerable without delegating"* — irreducibility, not horizon. ("Irreducibility" is this project's shorthand from the trading-env design note; `irreducib` returns 0 hits in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, so R16's phrasing is the one to quote.) Changing that would need a measurement neither paper supplies and our own store cannot yet give. Three mechanisms are worth porting as-is, all read from prime-agent source:

- `rlm()` returns a handle and **never** the child's answer; results arrive only through an explicit message or a file. It forces an explicit reply path instead of letting a child collapse into a completion.
- **Heterogeneous child models are a first-class primitive** (`model` = exact `provider/model` selector, `thinking` = child reasoning level, plus a bounded catalog search) — the direct analogue of our root-27B / leaf-35B-A3B split, and **nobody has evaluated it**. This is our gap and it is also everyone's.
- **Cost accounting aggregates root and descendants**, so a delegating arm cannot hide spend. Copy it with the asymmetry named: Prime Agent's own plotted cost includes subagents, and it never states whether any comparison harness spawns any. Ours must state it for every arm.

**Benchmark v2.** Four design constraints, each with a citation:

1. **Size the held-out split from a resolvable-margin target, not from budget.** AutoDesign's two comparators swap rank order between its 10-paper and 100-paper tracks under identical configuration. A 10-item track cannot resolve a few points.
2. **If v2 uses any per-task ceiling or hard cap, publish the full constant set and report the capped/uncapped decomposition.** AutoDesign discloses 1 of 11, applies them before averaging, and roughly half its flagship margin is the caps rather than the rubric. Our own margin rule should be reported as a decomposition, not a single number.
3. **Any screen that fires conditionally on batch size must be declared per-track.** AutoDesign's blinded style-homogeneity check runs on 100 posters and is off at 10, while Table 5 lists "frozen PosterBench protocol" as fixed across both.
4. **Whatever tunes the scorer must be disjoint from what it scores, and the split manifest must say so.** AutoDesign's judge prompt was A/B-validated on ten Main Track papers with three "must stay high" anchors, one of which is also in the 10-paper controlled set — and no disjointness statement between D_train/D_dev and the benchmark exists anywhere.

**The admission gate's evidence budget.** AutoDesign is the closest published analogue of a loop that edits its own harness, and **it answers the evidence-budget question by refusing to**: `|D_train|` and `|D_dev|` are NOT-FOUND in the paper, the appendix, Algorithm 1 and the code. Three things to copy verbatim — the dev split never shown to the proposer, exactly one named component changed per iteration for attributable credit, and comparison against the immediately preceding **accepted** state. Two things not to copy — no threshold and no interval, and **no negative control of any kind**. On the first, our lead is thinner than it looks and should not be claimed as one: we do have a threshold and an interval, and the gate's *cost* statistic has a characterised null floor (median 0.9670, CI [0.9212, 1.0670], `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-27-s6-lite-v0-artifact-gate.md:362`) — but the same spec records that **`K_CI_UPPER_MAX` has never bound a single verdict** (`:359`) and that **`K_CI_UPPER_MAX = 1.00` sits INSIDE the noise band** (`:364`). A threshold that has never bound and sits inside its own null band is a threshold on paper. The honest form is that we built the instrument AutoDesign did not and have not yet made it bite. **On that last point we have no standing to compare, and the claim this document previously made here was false in our own favour** [V]. The negative control was never exercised, and its input artifact **no longer exists**: `gate/artifacts/negative-control.{json,sha256}` was removed under reorganization **D1** — *"Duplicated gate inputs… live input directory for a runner that is gone; every byte is a verified duplicate of an archived record"*, disposition **"nothing"** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-01-reorganization.md:179`) — as a duplicate of `decisions/pc-*/candidate.json`. There is no `gate/` directory in the repo today and `find . -name "negative-control*"` returns nothing. Our position is not better in principle; **it is unbuilt**, and AutoDesign's absent negative control and ours are the same absence.

Our 2-rows-for-3-decisions hole (re-verified today: `docs/research/2026-08-27-s6-lite-v0/decisions/audit.jsonl` holds rows for pc-02 and pc-03 only, while pc-01 has a `decision.json` and a `decision.log` and reached no ledger) stays a hole. It is a **smaller** hole than either published analogue. AutoDesign publishes 54 acceptances with zero rejection count, zero per-update effect size and zero per-component attribution — having argued that its one-component rule *"keeps credit assignment interpretable"* — and does not release the record `L` that would carry any of it. Prime Agent runs auto-refine **on by default** and admits every artifact on a JSON-schema check alone, with a skill validator that never imports the module it names.

One thing to **add**, from prime-agent's `autonomous.ts`: a **no-work-no-retry primitive**. A failed gate is not re-run while the git worktree fingerprint is unchanged — *"not rerun: workspace unchanged since previous failed gate"* — alongside an explicit per-command attempt budget and a pass condition that is a host shell exit code. Our gate has an attempt discipline; it has no equivalent of "the workspace did not change, so this retry is free and does not count."

**Artifact authorship vs consumption.** AutoDesign is the **only** one of the seven papers now read where the agent **writes its own harness** — *"a coding agent that sequentially assumes the roles of a planner and a code editor… implements these changes in the current design harness H_t."* The mechanism exists. **The measurement does not.** Eq. 2's objective `J(H)` is defined purely over downstream artifact quality; no statistic about the authoring act is published anywhere — no accept/reject counts, no per-update effect size, no per-component attribution, no diff size — and the optimizer, the record and the 123 iteration checkpoints are absent from the release. Prime Agent measures **two** authorship-adjacent things, and neither is a measurement of authorship. Fig. 6's out-of-loop experiment counts rest on numerators of 3 to 25 hand-classified events with partly-estimated denominators, one of which is 3 against 3; and §3.3's nanoGPT run reports *"roughly ninety screening experiments and all 19 of its validated records"* over 85.5 hours — a raw count with **no comparison harness on the other side of it**, so it establishes that the loop produced records, not that the loop's authorship was worth anything.

So the gap **narrows and does not close: nobody has published an authorship measurement.** What the set now supplies is two independent observations that an *ungated* write path banks the wrong thing — Prime Agent's RCON specification exploit *"preserved as a reusable skill"* on a frontier model at a seven-day horizon, and our own spike's 8-of-8 `kind: memory` answer logs on a Q4_K_M 27B over four tasks. §9 S6's held-out rule (*"an artifact traceable to a held-out task voids the run"*) remains the only pre-registered instrument for authorship this document could find in any source, and its justification is now three-legged: our spike, Prime Agent's trace, and AutoDesign's 54 unattributed updates.

**The spike's own question, answered explicitly** (`docs/research/2026-08-27-prime-agent-spike.md` §5.3, cited in this document's header and until now only glanced at). §5.3's verdict was that given a free hand, a competent local root writes memories that **memorize the answers to the tasks it was just shown**: 8 of 8 refinements `kind: memory`, zero skill / prompt / subagent, procedure degrading across the sequence while answers do not, and one run in which the model's own memory carried a proven `re.M` regex it then declined to use. **Nothing in these three papers overturns that verdict.** Four things here are new relative to it:

1. **The same-model finding** (§5(a)). The spike compared a local root against itself and treated that as the disciplined choice; both published harness papers **also** hold the model fixed, so same-model baselines are not what distinguishes this project, and the spike's implicit claim to that distinction is retired.
2. **An author-declared null on a harness swap.** Prime Agent's own nanoGPT sentence — *"the choice of harness has little effect on final records compared to the noise of the experiment"* — is the spike's unstated worry stated by the people shipping the harness, on 18 runs across three models.
3. **A delegation shape to compare against.** Factorio's 633 depth-one subagents across 149 dispatch waves at **recursion depth 1**, where the spike produced no subagent artifact of any kind. That is a shape the spike could not have seen, and §6's caveats above apply to it in full.
4. **The RCON specification exploit *"preserved as a reusable skill"*.** This is the spike's `kind: memory` answer-log finding reproduced on a frontier model at a seven-day horizon — evidence that the failure is a property of **ungated write paths**, not of a Q4_K_M 27B, which is the generalization the spike explicitly could not make from n = 1 model.

What is **not** answered: §5.4's own self-criticism — swapping `./corpus.txt` between train turns inside one cwd taught the model something false about the world — is untouched by anything read here and is still owed on any repeat.

## 7. What this does NOT license

The flattering readings a hurried reader would take, each with the reason it fails:

- **Do not quote "AutoDesign's judge agrees with humans 51.9%, with 54 updates chained through it."** The 51.9% is PosterBench's; the 54 updates ran through R_meta; and the two share a rubric rather than being unrelated instruments. Fix the sentence in `2026-08-31-papers-verified.md` §9 rather than repeating it.
- **Do not map the 52–74% ladder onto AutoDesign's +7.45.** Per-pair agreement against a mean of 100. The valid claim is about single close comparisons.
- **Do not cite the native/foreign separation as evidence of agent fit.** It is our taxonomy, collinear with baseline (r = −0.842), non-monotone, degraded under headroom normalisation, inverted across tracks, and dominated by a wrapper effect the paper itself measures at 17.98 points.
- **Do not claim same-model baselines as this project's differentiator.** Both papers hold the model fixed. What is left to claim is one clean item and two qualified ones — see §5(a), and read the next four bullets before quoting any of them.
- **Do not claim matched budget as a live differentiator.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:384` deliberately runs no cost gate, S4's arms were not budget-matched, and the rule was adopted on 2026-08-31 for comparisons not yet run. It is **owed, not held** — and it was never pre-registered, in this repo or anywhere else.
- **Do not attach `median 0.9670, CI [0.9212, 1.0670]` to any benchmark score.** It is the S6-lite gate's K-ratio null floor over a **cost** statistic, from two OFF replications. The benchmark's success-rate deltas have **no** characterised null floor, and saying otherwise merges two instruments in our favour.
- **Do not quote +30 vs B1 as a current margin.** The Vulkan re-run makes it **+14** and publishes no interval at all. The interval that still holds is +13 vs B2, CI [+0.256, +0.589].
- **Do not say our negative control "exists but has never been exercised."** It was never exercised **and** its input artifact is gone (reorganization D1). AutoDesign has no negative control and neither do we; that row is a tie, not a lead.
- **Do not read Factorio beside our 237/15/6 as a bracket on horizon.** n = 1, curated, frontier model, different environment — and our side of it is faulted under R16's `slot_pool_error_drained`, which makes delegation's quality unmeasured rather than bad. It is a hypothesis, and it licenses no benchmark-v2 design constraint by itself.
- **Do not read Prime Agent's 30% → 95.5% as a harness effect.** The paper disowns the causal reading in its own body text, the baseline is external, and the authors' matched-prompt control was run and withheld.
- **Do not read Prime Agent's 8.6% GLM row as support for F2.** All four models are frontier API models and there is no parameter or quantization axis anywhere in the paper, so it cannot adjudicate a claim about quantized small roots in either direction. Do not reach for the multiplier either: §3.1 shows the "the scaffold roughly doubles the score" reading is an artifact of pairing each model against a *different* baseline harness. Hold the harness fixed and the ordering reverses — 11.19× for the weaker model against 3.16× for the stronger — which is the reading a critic would turn against us.
- **Do not read Prime Agent's 20-of-27 as a harness win at large.** That is a sign count. Four of 27 clear a 7-point-equivalent floor, three of those four sit in the Pi-mono column, and the paper attributes that column to model/harness co-training.
- **Do not treat AutoDesign as an authorship measurement.** It is an authorship *mechanism* with a *consumption* measurement attached.
- **Do not treat AutoDesign's Eq. 6 as validated by its results.** There is no H₀-vs-H_T row, no placebo-update arm and no gate-disabled arm anywhere. The +12.40 measures having a design harness.
- **Do not treat AutoDesign's Fig. 1(a) as a self-improvement curve.** n = 1, evaluator unnamed, its four values appear nowhere in the text, and its final leg is the human-in-the-loop stage that is also where the paper permits R_meta to be revised.
- **Do not treat "code holds the authoritative state while the model does the inexact part" as validated by Code World Model.** It is a design choice argued from cost intuition, its motivating experiment is unreported, and the paper's own Fig. 2 shows three writers into world state including the generative model itself.
- **Do not cite Code World Model for anything numeric.** There is nothing to cite.
- **Do not treat either repository as reproducing its paper.** AutoDesign ships the design harness and not the meta-harness; prime-agent ships the harness and not one benchmark. Neither ships a score archive.
- **Do not treat any CONFIRMED above as true.** It means *the paper says this*, or *the code does this*. All three are v1, unreviewed, uncorroborated.

## 8. What remains unverified

**Code World Model.** No source repository, no weights, no data release — a project page only, and the paper's sole prior-work comparison lives on it and **was not fetched**. No held-out set and no evaluation protocol of any kind exists to attach a metric to. Appendix A names three deliberately isolating probe conditions (*"isolating camera following from entity translation"*, *"testing fine motion and scene preservation without large displacement"*) whose outputs appear nowhere in the paper — three designed probes, zero reported outcomes. The injection mechanism for the 124-frame proxy condition is never stated. The base MiniMax-H3 parameter count is never given, so the ≈596 M LoRA parameters cannot be expressed as a fraction. **The game engine and the programming language are never named** — `Unity`, `Unreal`, `Godot`, `Blender`, `three.js`, `C++`, `C#`, `JavaScript` and `TypeScript` return 0 hits each, and `Python`'s three hits are all in §2.3 related work describing other people's systems — so the thesis sentence *"a coding agent governs long-horizon world evolution through executable code"* has no artifact a reader could ask to see. **And there is no agent-call accounting of any kind**: no invocation count, no reasoning frequency, no per-call latency, no cost for either side of the agent/code split, despite that split being the paper's central architectural claim. That second absence is the one this project most wanted — it is exactly the per-call number our own delegation gap needs, and the nearest prior art making the same bet does not produce it, so it has to be measured here.
